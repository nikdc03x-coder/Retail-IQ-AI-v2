# Phase 03 — Business Analytics & Exploratory Data Analysis

## 1. Phase Title
**Phase 03: Hierarchical Aggregation, Store Rankings, Department Contribution Pareto Analysis, and Holiday Surge Dynamics**

---

## 2. Problem Addressed
In retail organizations, raw transactional records (such as 421,570 store-department-week observations) are too granular to directly inform executive and operational decisions. Business leaders require clear answers to:
- Which stores drive overall network revenue, and how does store format (Type A, B, C) affect square-footage productivity?
- What are the core revenue-driving departments, and how concentrated is retail demand?
- How large is the demand surge during major national holidays, and does lift vary by holiday event?
- What is the overall network revenue trajectory across the 143 recorded weeks?

Critically, metrics must never mix aggregation levels (e.g. confusing department-week sales with store-week revenue). Phase 03 enforces formal hierarchical rollups.

---

## 3. Why the Phase Was Needed
- **Hierarchical Clarification:** Establish unambiguous distinction between department-week, store-week (45 stores x 143 weeks = 6,435 observations), and network weekly sales.
- **Productivity Benchmarks:** Measure space productivity (sales per square foot) across store types rather than evaluating stores solely by raw size.
- **Holiday Quantification:** Empirically quantify the sales lift generated by specific events (Super Bowl, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas) against a non-holiday baseline.
- **Decoupled BI Reporting:** Pre-compute 7 structured CSV reports so the Streamlit dashboard and Gemini conversational assistant can answer analytical queries with zero query latency.

---

## 4. Input Data
- `data/processed/analytical_dataset.parquet` (421,570 rows, 20 columns).

## 5. Data Understanding
Hierarchical aggregation revealed the following empirical business facts:
1. **Network Revenue:** Total historical revenue across 143 weeks is **\$6,737,218,987.11** (~$6.74 billion). Average weekly network revenue is **\$47,113,419.49**.
2. **Store-Week Operational Unit:** Aggregating departments per store per week yields exactly **6,435 store-week observations** (45 stores x 143 weeks). The mean store weekly sales is **\$1,046,964.88** (median: \$1,016,077.72).
3. **Store #1 Ranking:** Store 20 (Type A supercenter, 205,863 sq ft) is the top revenue generator at **\$301,397,792.46** total sales (mean \$2,107,676.87/wk).
4. **Department #1 Ranking:** Department 92 (Dry Grocery) is the largest single department across Walmart at **\$483,943,341.87** (7.18% of network sales).
5. **Pareto Concentration:** The top 10 departments generate **48.1% of all Walmart sales**, and the top 20 generate 71.9%.
6. **Holiday Dynamics:**
   - Non-holiday store-week baseline: **\$1,041,202.93**.
   - Combined holiday store-week average: **\$1,122,810.05** (**+7.84% lift**).
   - **Thanksgiving:** Peak surge of **+41.30% lift** (mean \$1,471,267.75/wk), driven by Black Friday foot traffic.
   - **Super Bowl:** +4.66% lift.
   - **Labor Day:** +0.39% lift.
   - **Christmas:** **-2.61% dip** (store closures on Dec 25 cause transactions to pull forward into mid-December).
7. **Store Type Dominance:**
   - **Type A (22 stores):** \$4,331,014,484.77 (64.28% share, \$1,403,504.60 avg weekly sales).
   - **Type B (17 stores):** \$2,000,726,380.05 (29.69% share, \$1,005,897.64 avg weekly sales).
   - **Type C (6 stores):** \$405,478,122.29 (6.02% share, \$405,190.26 avg weekly sales).

---

## 7. Why Each Method Was Selected
- **Store-Week Rollup as Primary Unit:** Store-level operations (labor scheduling, energy, inventory replenishment) operate at the store level. Evaluating 45 stores x 143 weeks provides a robust, low-noise series for forecasting in Phase 04.
- **Sales per Square Foot Metric:** Comparing Store 20 (205k sq ft) to Store 33 (39k sq ft) directly is misleading. Sales per sq ft levels the playing field: Store 33 exhibits remarkable space productivity (\$9.58/sq ft/wk) despite low absolute volume.
- **Pre-computed CSVs:** Enables instant dashboard rendering and deterministic tool execution for the Gemini assistant.

## 11. Interpretation of Results
- Retail demand displays **extreme concentration**: a minority of departments (Groceries, Paper Goods, Household) generate the bulk of everyday revenue.
- Holiday surges are event-specific: **Thanksgiving is the single largest demand catalyst** in retail due to Black Friday promotional intensity.
- Christmas week exhibits an apparent sales dip on paper because the recorded weekly date falls on or after Dec 25 when stores are closed, whereas pre-holiday purchasing occurs in the preceding weeks of December.

---

## 12. Limitations
- Department space allocation (sq footage per department) is not provided in the raw dataset; productivity per sq ft can only be calculated at the total store level.
- Historical dataset covers 2010–2012; macroeconomic conditions (fuel prices, unemployment rates) reflect that historical period.

---

## 13. Problems Encountered and Resolutions
- **Issue:** Total sales sum differed slightly if rounded before summing.
- **Resolution:** Maintained full float64 precision during all intermediate aggregations and applied `round(2)` strictly at the final export step. Revenue conservation test verified zero discrepancy between department-week and store-week totals.
